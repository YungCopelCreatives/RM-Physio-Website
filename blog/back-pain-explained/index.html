Understanding Lower Back Pain: Causes, Treatment & Prevention

Published: January 15, 2025 • 8 min read • By Temošo Nkadimeng, Physiotherapist

Key Takeaways

  • Lower back pain affects 80% of people at some point in their lives
  • Most cases are mechanical and respond well to physiotherapy
  • Early intervention leads to better outcomes and prevents chronicity
  • Lifestyle factors play a crucial role in both causes and prevention
  • Exercise and movement are key components of treatment and prevention

Introduction

Lower back pain is one of the most common complaints we see in physiotherapy practice. It affects people of all ages and backgrounds, from office workers to elite athletes. As a physiotherapist practicing in Pretoria East, I've helped hundreds of patients overcome back pain and return to their active lives. In this comprehensive guide, I'll explain everything you need to know about lower back pain - from what causes it to how you can treat and prevent it.

Understanding the Lower Back

Your lower back, or lumbar spine, consists of five vertebrae (L1-L5) stacked on top of each other. Between each vertebra are intervertebral discs that act as shock absorbers. The spine is supported by numerous muscles, ligaments, and other soft tissues. This complex system allows for movement while supporting the weight of your upper body.

Common Causes of Lower Back Pain

1. Mechanical Causes

  • Muscle Strain: The most common cause, often from lifting, twisting, or sudden movements
  • Poor Posture: Prolonged sitting, slouching, or forward head posture
  • Disc Problems: Herniation, bulges, or degeneration
  • Facet Joint Dysfunction: Irritation of the small joints in the spine
  • Spinal Stenosis: Narrowing of the spinal canal

2. Lifestyle Factors

  • Sedentary lifestyle and lack of exercise
  • Poor ergonomics at work
  • Obesity and excess weight
  • Smoking (affects disc nutrition)
  • Stress and psychological factors

Treatment Approaches

Physiotherapy Treatment

As a physiotherapist, I use evidence-based treatments that have been proven effective for lower back pain:

  • Manual Therapy: Hands-on techniques to improve mobility and reduce pain
  • Exercise Therapy: Specific exercises to strengthen and stabilize the spine
  • Education: Understanding your condition and how to manage it
  • Movement Training: Learning proper body mechanics and posture

Home Management

  • Stay active and avoid prolonged bed rest
  • Use heat or ice as appropriate
  • Practice gentle stretching and mobility exercises
  • Maintain good posture throughout the day
  • Manage stress through relaxation techniques

Prevention Strategies

Exercise and Fitness

Regular exercise is one of the best ways to prevent back pain. Focus on:

  • Core strengthening exercises
  • Flexibility and mobility work
  • Cardiovascular fitness
  • Functional movement training

Workplace Ergonomics

  • Adjust your chair height and monitor position
  • Take regular breaks from sitting
  • Use proper lifting techniques
  • Consider a standing desk or ergonomic accessories

When to Seek Professional Help

You should consult a physiotherapist if you experience:

  • Pain that persists for more than a few days
  • Pain that interferes with daily activities
  • Radiating pain into your legs
  • Numbness, tingling, or weakness
  • Recurring episodes of back pain

Red Flags - Seek Immediate Medical Attention:

  • Loss of bladder or bowel control
  • Progressive weakness in legs
  • Severe pain following trauma
  • Fever with back pain
  • Night pain that wakes you from sleep

Conclusion

Lower back pain is a common but highly treatable condition. With the right approach combining professional treatment, home management, and prevention strategies, most people can achieve significant improvement and return to their normal activities. The key is early intervention and taking an active approach to your recovery.
