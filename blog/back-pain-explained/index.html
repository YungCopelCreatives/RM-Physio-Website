Understanding Lower Back Pain: Causes, Treatment & Prevention

By Temošo Nkadimeng, physiotherapist · January 15, 2025 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Lower back pain affects approximately 80% of individuals at some point in their lives
  • Most cases are mechanical in nature and respond exceptionally well to targeted physiotherapy
  • Early professional intervention leads to faster recovery and prevents pain from becoming chronic
  • Sedentary desk posture and weak core stabilisers are primary contributing factors
  • Active movement, gentle loading, and targeted exercises outperform prolonged bed rest every time

Lower back pain is one of the most common complaints encountered in clinical physiotherapy practice. It affects individuals of all ages and walks of life, from desk-bound knowledge workers in corporate offices to elite competitive athletes. Practicing in Centurion, I have helped hundreds of patients conquer persistent lumbar pain and regain their mobility.

Your lower back, or lumbar spine, consists of five vertebrae (L1-L5) stacked vertically. In between each vertebral body sit specialised intervertebral discs that function as dynamic shock absorbers. This architectural marvel is supported by extensive ligamentous networks and muscular slings, including the transverse abdominis, multifidus, and gluteal musculature.

Most episodes of back pain arise from mechanical issues: acute muscle spasms or ligament sprains from sudden lifting, prolonged sustained slouching stressing posterior spinal elements, facet joint irritation, or disc bulges irritating nearby nerve roots. Lifestyle factors such as prolonged sitting, high stress levels, and lack of core endurance further magnify symptoms.

Evidence-based physiotherapy treatment combines manual therapy to restore joint play, dry needling to deactivate persistent myofascial trigger points, targeted progressive core stabilisation exercises, and movement mechanics re-education. Staying gently active rather than taking prolonged bed rest preserves disc nutrition and promotes tissue healing.

When to seek urgent medical care

  • Loss of bowel or bladder control (seek emergency medical attention immediately)
  • Progressive weakness, foot drop, or numbness in the saddle/groin region
  • Unrelenting night pain that wakes you up from sound sleep
  • High fever or chills accompanying acute back pain
