Understanding Sciatica

Learn about sciatic nerve pain and how physiotherapy can provide effective relief

Sciatica refers to pain that radiates along the path of the sciatic nerve, which branches from your lower back through your hips and buttocks and down each leg. Typically, sciatica affects only one side of your body and can range from mild discomfort to severe, debilitating pain that interferes with daily activities.

Key Facts About Sciatica

  • Affects approximately 40% of people during their lifetime
  • Most episodes resolve within 4-6 weeks with proper treatment
  • Conservative physiotherapy is the first-line treatment
  • Early intervention prevents chronic pain development
  • Most cases don't require surgery when treated properly

Symptoms & Common Causes

Recognizing sciatica and understanding what might be causing your symptoms

⚡

Common Symptoms

How sciatica typically presents

▼

Primary Symptoms:

  • Radiating Pain: Sharp, shooting pain from lower back down the leg
  • Numbness & Tingling: Pins and needles sensation in leg or foot
  • Muscle Weakness: Difficulty moving foot or toes
  • Burning Sensation: Burning or electric shock-like feelings

Pain Patterns:

  • Usually affects one leg (unilateral)
  • May worsen when sitting, coughing, or sneezing
  • Often better when walking or lying down
  • Can range from mild ache to severe, debilitating pain
🦴

Disc-Related Causes

Spinal disc problems that can cause sciatica

▼

Disc Conditions:

  • Herniated Disc: Disc material pressing on nerve roots
  • Bulging Disc: Disc protrusion irritating surrounding nerves
  • Degenerative Disc Disease: Age-related disc changes causing instability
  • Disc Protrusion: Partial displacement of disc material

Common Locations:

  • L4-L5 disc level (most common)
  • L5-S1 disc level
  • Multiple level involvement (less common)
  • Symptoms vary based on specific nerve root affected
🔬

Other Causes

Non-disc related causes of sciatic symptoms

▼

Structural Causes:

  • Piriformis Syndrome: Muscle spasm compressing sciatic nerve
  • Spinal Stenosis: Narrowing of spinal canal
  • Spondylolisthesis: Vertebra slipping forward
  • Facet Joint Dysfunction: Joint irritation and inflammation

Risk Factors:

  • Age (peak incidence 40-50 years)
  • Prolonged sitting or sedentary lifestyle
  • Heavy lifting with poor technique
  • Diabetes and obesity
  • Previous back injuries
🚨

Red Flag Symptoms

Warning signs that require immediate medical attention

▼

Seek Emergency Care For:

  • Cauda Equina Syndrome: Loss of bowel/bladder control
  • Progressive Weakness: Rapidly worsening leg weakness
  • Bilateral Symptoms: Symptoms in both legs
  • Severe Neurological Loss: Complete loss of sensation or movement

Seek Prompt Medical Care For:

  • Severe pain not responding to rest or position changes
  • Progressive numbness or weakness
  • Difficulty walking due to leg weakness
  • Pain following significant trauma

Our Evidence-Based Treatment Approach

Comprehensive physiotherapy program to relieve sciatic pain and prevent recurrence

1

Comprehensive Assessment

Duration: 60 minutes

  • Detailed History: Pain patterns, onset, aggravating/easing factors
  • Neurological Examination: Reflexes, sensation, muscle strength testing
  • Spinal Assessment: Movement analysis and palpation
  • Special Tests: Straight leg raise, neural tension tests
  • Functional Assessment: Walking, sitting, and movement patterns
  • Red Flag Screening: Ruling out serious spinal conditions
2

Acute Pain Management

Focus: Reduce nerve irritation and manage pain

  • Position of Comfort: Teaching positions that reduce nerve tension
  • Gentle Mobilization: Carefully graded spinal movements
  • Neural Mobilization: Gentle techniques to reduce nerve sensitivity
  • Manual Therapy: Soft tissue work and joint mobilization
  • Pain Education: Understanding your condition and recovery process
  • Activity Modification: Guidelines for daily activities and work
3

Mobility Restoration

Focus: Improve spinal movement and reduce stiffness

  • Directional Preference Exercises: McKenzie method if appropriate
  • Spinal Mobilization: Joint mobilization techniques
  • Flexibility Exercises: Targeted stretches for tight muscles
  • Postural Correction: Addressing faulty movement patterns
  • Neural Gliding: Exercises to improve nerve mobility
4

Strengthening & Stabilization

Focus: Build strength and stability to prevent recurrence

  • Core Strengthening: Deep abdominal and spinal stabilizer exercises
  • Hip Strengthening: Glutes and hip stabilizer exercises
  • Spinal Strengthening: Progressive back muscle strengthening
  • Functional Training: Movement patterns for daily activities
  • Balance and Proprioception: Improving movement control
5

Return to Activity

Focus: Safe return to work, sport, and recreational activities

  • Graduated Loading: Progressive increase in activity levels
  • Work Conditioning: Preparing for occupational demands
  • Sport-Specific Training: Return-to-sport protocols when appropriate
  • Movement Re-education: Optimizing lifting and bending techniques
  • Endurance Training: Building tolerance for prolonged activities
6

Prevention & Long-term Management

  • Maintenance Exercise Program: Ongoing exercises to prevent recurrence
  • Ergonomic Assessment: Workplace and home setup optimization
  • Lifestyle Modification: Activity pacing and load management
  • Self-Management Skills: Techniques for managing minor flare-ups
  • Follow-up Care: Monitoring progress and adjusting program

Self-Care & Management Strategies

Evidence-based techniques you can use at home to manage sciatica symptoms

🛏️

Comfortable Positioning

  • ✓ Sleep on side with pillow between knees
  • ✓ Reclined position in chair for sitting
  • ✓ Avoid prolonged sitting or standing
  • ✓ Use lumbar support when sitting
  • ✓ Take frequent position changes
❄️

Pain Relief Techniques

  • ✓ Ice for acute pain and inflammation
  • ✓ Heat for muscle spasms and stiffness
  • ✓ Gentle walking as tolerated
  • ✓ Avoid bed rest for extended periods
  • ✓ Relaxation and breathing techniques
🧘

Gentle Exercises

  • ✓ Knee-to-chest stretches
  • ✓ Gentle spinal movements as tolerated
  • ✓ Pelvic tilts in lying position
  • ✓ Deep breathing exercises
  • ✓ Light walking when able
⚠️

Activities to Avoid

  • ✗ Heavy lifting or bending
  • ✗ Sudden twisting movements
  • ✗ High-impact activities
  • ✗ Prolonged sitting in poor posture
  • ✗ Activities that increase leg pain

Recovery Timeline & Prognosis

What to expect during your recovery from sciatica

Typical Recovery Timeline

  • Week 1-2: Focus on pain management and gentle movement
  • Week 3-4: Significant improvement in pain and function expected
  • Week 6-8: Most patients see substantial improvement
  • 3-6 months: Full recovery and return to all activities

Success Rate: 80-90% of sciatica cases improve significantly with conservative physiotherapy treatment.

When to Seek Help

  • Severe leg pain that doesn't improve with rest
  • Numbness or weakness in the leg
  • Pain interfering with sleep or daily activities
  • Symptoms persisting beyond 2 weeks
  • Previous episodes that keep recurring

Don't Suffer in Silence

Sciatica can be effectively treated with physiotherapy. Early intervention leads to faster recovery and prevents chronic pain development. Our experienced physiotherapist will create a personalized treatment plan to get you back to pain-free living.
